# BSC-Forensics

## Historical Consensus & State-Transition Differential Testing Framework

BSC-Forensics is an open-source research and developer-infrastructure project for reproducible analysis of historical BNB Smart Chain behavior.

The project is designed to support structured inspection of historical consensus-related behavior, state-transition execution, differential testing, and evidence-oriented research workflows.

---

## Overview

The purpose of this repository is to provide a structured framework for examining historical blockchain behavior through reproducible procedures and documented research artifacts.

The repository separates source data, validation procedures, analytical methods, observations, and interpretation so that individual stages can be inspected independently.

---

## Research Scope

The research scope includes:

- Historical blockchain data analysis
- Consensus-related behavior
- State-transition execution
- Differential testing
- Structural inspection
- Statistical analysis
- Reproducible research workflows
- Evidence preservation and documentation

The scope of an individual experiment is defined by its corresponding research artifact and documented methodology.

---

## Methodology

### Data Acquisition

Relevant historical blockchain records are collected from defined data sources while preserving the original analytical scope.

### Validation

Input data and derived artifacts are checked for completeness, consistency, and reproducibility before analysis.

### State-Transition Analysis

State-transition behavior is examined through structured inspection of consensus-critical execution paths where applicable.

### Differential Testing

Controlled comparisons may be performed across implementations, execution paths, or defined test conditions.

### Statistical Analysis

Quantitative methods may be applied to evaluate observed patterns, deviations, or other measurable properties.

Statistical results are treated as analytical evidence and are not, by themselves, attribution.

---

## Reproducibility

Research procedures are structured so that an independent researcher can inspect the methodology, reproduce applicable processing steps, and compare resulting outputs.

Reproduction should use the documented data scope, procedures, and research artifacts associated with each experiment.

---

## Evidence & Artifacts

Research artifacts may include:

- Source datasets
- Derived datasets
- Validation records
- Analysis scripts
- Test outputs
- Hashes and integrity records
- Methodology documentation
- Reproduction notes

Artifacts should remain traceable to the analytical procedure that produced them.

---

## Validation Status

Validation status is reported separately from analytical interpretation.

Where applicable, validation records document:

- Data completeness
- Internal consistency
- Reproduction status
- Independent verification
- Test execution status

No causal or attributional conclusion should be inferred solely from a validation status.

---

## Evidence Separation

The project maintains a conceptual separation between:

1. Observed blockchain data
2. Analytical results
3. Reproduction and validation results
4. Interpretation
5. Attribution hypotheses

This separation is intended to prevent analytical observations from being presented as causal or attributional conclusions without additional evidence.

---

## Limitations

Results are dependent on the defined data scope, source availability, implementation versions, analytical procedures, and experimental conditions.

A statistical or differential-testing result does not independently establish causation, intent, authorship, or attribution.

Conclusions should therefore remain limited to what the documented evidence and methodology support.

---

## Repository Structure

The repository may contain research code, analysis procedures, validation artifacts, documentation, and reproducibility materials.

The structure may evolve as additional experiments and research artifacts are added.

---

## Independent Verification

Independent researchers are encouraged to inspect the documented methodology and available research artifacts and reproduce applicable procedures independently.

Independent reproduction should be reported separately from the original analytical results.
